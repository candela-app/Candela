Follow one story through Candela

A patient arrives at Candela, meets a doctor or starts independently, practises games, and builds a history over time. Along the way, a trial may become a subscription, a doctor may change, or a hospital may reassign patients.

The diagrams are arranged in that order. Read each Process for a short guide to the flow, then read Situation as a small real-world story showing the flow in action. Together, the chapters explain how the pieces connect.

How to follow the story
  1. Follow the chapters in order: platform and roles, clinic setup, patient onboarding, practice, dashboards and what a score means, access changes, and relationship changes.
  2. Read Process for a short guide to the arrows and decisions in each diagram.
  3. Read Situation as a small story. It keeps the original example and shows the business flow in action.
  4. The dashboard chapter explains what each role sees. The final chapters connect the business concepts and collect the rules that must remain true across the whole journey.
  5. Open business decisions are listed at the end; these are not finalized requirements.

Part 5 — What each dashboard shows

Now that the patient journey is clear, the dashboards become the role-specific windows into that same journey. Each role sees the information and actions needed for its responsibilities.

One platform, four views

The dashboards should not be four unrelated products. They should show the same underlying business events from four different perspectives: the patient manages their own practice and subscription; the doctor manages authorized patients and prescriptions; the hospital administrator manages organization operations; and the Super Admin manages platform-wide operations and subscriptions.

The images below are illustrative dashboard wireframes to show content hierarchy, not final UI designs or live product screenshots. Example counts are placeholders, not real Candela data.

PPatient dashboard

Illustrative patient dashboard: practice entry point, access status, recent progress, and available games.

What belongs here

  • Access status: free-trial days remaining or subscription status, with a clear action to manage or renew.
  • Practice entry: games and levels currently permitted by the patient's plan and, for doctor-managed patients, the prescription.
  • Progress overview: completed sessions and recent game-specific progress using clearly defined metrics.
  • Session history: completed plays, dates, game names, and available results; filters should not combine unrelated metrics.
  • Account tools: profile, settings, support, and subscription management.
  • Expired state: keep the account and subscription screen accessible, explain that gameplay is locked, and do not imply that no practice happened when access was blocked.

Situation

After logging in, Ananya sees that her trial has three days left, the games she can currently play, and the results of her recent completed sessions. When the trial expires, she can still sign in and open subscription management, but the dashboard clearly explains why practice is locked. Her past records are retained.

DDoctor dashboard

Illustrative doctor dashboard: assigned-patient overview, prescription workload, and transfer requests.

What belongs here

  • Assigned patient list: search and filter patients the doctor is currently authorized to access.
  • Patient detail: continuous timeline, completed-session history, game-specific progress, and relevant prescription context.
  • Prescription management: create or update prescribed games and levels, with effective status and dates.
  • Transfer requests: incoming requests to accept or reject, with clear patient and request status.
  • Access context: trial or subscription status where relevant to interpreting practice activity; doctors do not pay for the platform and should not receive hospital billing controls.
  • Analytics: filter by game, date range, prescription, and practice type; show comparable metrics and avoid unsupported clinical claims.

Situation

Dr. Rao opens a patient's record before a review. He checks the current prescription, filters the timeline to sessions completed under that prescription, and notices a gap in practice. The dashboard distinguishes the gap from a period when subscription access was locked. A transfer request from another patient appears in a separate queue.

HHospital Admin dashboard

Illustrative organization dashboard: staff and patient counts, activity overview, and controlled reassignment workflow.

What belongs here

  • Organization overview: doctors, patient assignments, and relevant operational activity within the administrator's organization.
  • Doctor management: roster, active/inactive status, onboarding and offboarding workflows.
  • Patient assignment management: authorized patient groupings and current doctor assignments.
  • Bulk reassignment: select departing doctor, choose an eligible replacement, preview affected patients, provide a reason, confirm, and review results.
  • Operational reporting: adoption and activity summaries that are supported by available data.
  • Scope limits: no hospital billing dashboard; no silent cross-organization reassignment; no unrestricted clinical-history access just because someone is an administrator.

Situation

A doctor is leaving the hospital. The administrator opens the roster, selects the departing doctor, reviews the affected patients, chooses an eligible replacement, and confirms the reassignment with a reason. The dashboard reports which assignments succeeded, preserves patient history, and records the operation for audit.

SSuper Admin dashboard

Illustrative platform operations dashboard: organizations, subscription status, payment exceptions, and audit/support queues.

What belongs here

  • Platform overview: organizations and account totals, plus operational health indicators supported by real data.
  • Subscription operations: patient subscription states, trials, renewals, cancellations, and conversions where implemented.
  • Payments and revenue: payment status, exceptions, refunds, and revenue reporting only after a payment provider and billing model are decided.
  • Plan management: plan and entitlement administration once pricing and plan tiers are finalized.
  • Support and audit: operational support queues, important administrative actions, and traceable changes.
  • Privacy boundary: platform operations do not automatically grant unrestricted access to patient clinical history.

Situation

The Super Admin notices an increase in failed renewals and opens the payment operations view to review affected subscriptions and support cases. They can investigate platform and billing status, but they do not automatically gain unrestricted access to patients' practice details. Pricing, plan tiers, and payment provider remain decisions to finalize.

Dashboard rules shared across Candela

  • Every dashboard must enforce role and relationship permissions in the backend, not only hide buttons in the interface.
  • Clearly distinguish “no recent practice” from “practice unavailable because access is locked.”
  • Preserve patient identity and historical sessions when subscriptions, doctors, or organizations change.
  • Only show metrics with a defined meaning; do not aggregate unlike games or describe scores as diagnosis or proof of clinical improvement.
  • Do not invent revenue, conversion, or payment metrics before the required billing data exists.

13From a finished game to a score

Process

Follow one finished game. A score is the correct responses divided by the responses that count, then shown as a percent. Bee Path is the exception: it keeps a path score, not a tap score. Leaving a game early does not create a score.

Swipe sideways to see the whole diagram.

flowchart TD
    Play[Patient plays a game] --> Finish{Did the patient finish?}
    Finish -->|Left early| Skip[Nothing is saved]
    Finish -->|Finished| Saved[One saved session]
    Saved --> Card[Game card counts every tap type for that game]
    Saved --> Level[Level page uses that game's own tap rules]
    Saved --> Bee[Bee Path keeps path adherence]

Situation

A patient finishes Rotatory. They hit the called letter 20 times and a different letter 10 times. They do not tap the empty wheel, and nothing times out. Correct means a hit on the right target. A wrong tap means a hit on something that was showing but was not the target. A miss means a tap on empty space or the wheel. A timeout means the target ended before a correct hit. The attempts are the taps on the bottom of the fraction.

On the game card the fraction uses every tap: 20 ÷ (20 + 10 + 0 + 0) = 20 ÷ 30 = 66.7%. The doctor looking at that same game also sees 66.7%. The 20 and the 10 are not added together and then divided by some other number.

The patient plays twice more. The first sitting is 9 correct and 1 wrong, so 90% of 10 taps. The second is 1 correct and 1 wrong, so 50% of 2 taps. Averaging 90 and 50 would say 70, and that would treat the short sitting as equal to the long one. The chart adds the taps first: (9 + 1) ÷ (10 + 2) = 10 ÷ 12 = 83.3%. A family of games does not receive one combined score.

A later Rotatory sitting has 1 correct hit and 9 timeouts. The game card still counts the timeouts, so 1 ÷ 10 = 10%. The level page for Rotatory, Sorting, and Bubble Chase does not count timeouts, so that same sitting is 1 ÷ 1 = 100%. Location Memory, Direction Sense, and Familiar Faces also leave misses off the level page. Seeing 10% on the card and 100% on the level is expected. A sitting with no taps shows 0% on the card, never 100%, and the level page says a score is not available.

Bee Path is different. The score is path adherence: how closely the trace followed the path. It is not a count of taps. One session is 100% across 2 finished rounds, so it counts twice. The next is 50% across 1 finished round, so it counts once. (100 × 2 + 50 × 1) ÷ 3 = 83.3%. The label says Path adherence. If the number of rounds does not match the number completed, that session counts once.

Reaction time is how long the correct response took after the target appeared. Wrong taps are not included. If each correct time was saved, the average uses those times. If the list is empty and the saved average is 1.5 seconds, the screen keeps 1.5 seconds. Speed and accuracy together is the accuracy percent divided by that average in seconds. An 80% sitting with a 2.0 second average is 80 ÷ 2 = 40. This describes the sitting. It does not diagnose the patient.

Time to finish is how long the sitting took. Pursuit and Bubble Chase instead add the time of each trial, so a total that includes them is adding trial times, not continuous minutes in the app.

CorrectA response on the right target.
Wrong tapA response on the wrong target that was showing.
MissA tap on empty space, the background, or the wheel.
TimeoutThe target ended before a correct response.
AttemptsThe responses on the bottom of the score. The game card includes all of them. Some level pages leave timeouts out, and a few also leave misses out.
AccuracyCorrect divided by attempts, shown as a percent with one decimal. 20 correct and 10 wrong is 66.7%.
Added-together scoreCounts from several sittings are added before dividing. They are not an average of the percentages.

14What the patient and the doctor each see

Process

A finished session can belong to the prescription that was active at that time. The home card counts every saved session and does not show a score. History, charts, and the practice summary use the newest 2,000 sessions when more than that exist, and they say so.

Swipe sideways to see the whole diagram.

flowchart TD
    Saved[Finished session] --> Linked{Was the prescription saved with it?}
    Linked -->|Yes, and it matches| Counted[Count it on that assignment]
    Linked -->|Yes, but it does not match| Aside[Leave it unlinked]
    Linked -->|No| Cover{Does one assignment cover that time?}
    Cover -->|Yes| Counted
    Cover -->|No| Aside
    Counted --> Dates[Keep sessions inside the chosen dates]
    Dates --> Many{More than 2,000 in that range?}
    Many -->|Yes| Newest[Show the newest 2,000 and say older ones were left out]
    Many -->|No| AllRows[Show all of them]
    Newest --> Views[Patient sees current assignments. Doctor also sees former assignments and unlinked sessions]
    AllRows --> Views

Situation

The home card tells the patient how many sessions have been saved, and the game and level of the newest one. It does not show 66.7% or any other score. History and charts use the newest 2,000 saved sessions. If older ones were left out, the screen says the totals are incomplete. A day counts as active when it has at least one saved session. A streak is consecutive days that still include today or yesterday. When more than 2,000 sessions exist, the streak uses only those newest 2,000. Gaze Hold is not saved, so it never appears here.

The doctor's prescription is kept as a snapshot: which games and levels were assigned, and from when. A session saved with that snapshot stays with it. If the session does not fit that snapshot, it stays unlinked and is not quietly moved to a later prescription. A session saved without a snapshot can still be counted when one assignment covers its time. A finished game that was not assigned is shown to the doctor as recorded but not assigned. The patient sees the assignments that are current, and how many sessions were saved on the chosen dates. The doctor also sees levels that used to be assigned, the unassigned sessions, and the unlinked ones. A session that falls exactly on the end of the chosen dates is outside that range. More than 2,000 sessions in the range keep the newest 2,000, and the screen says the older ones were left out.

Part 9 — Game categories and hospital-entered measurements

This high-level plan adds the agreed navigation and records requirements while leaving the detailed measurement-entry design open for later discussion.

13Normal and Pediatric Games and Prescriptions

Process

The Games area provides separate navigation tabs or pages for Normal Games and Pediatric Games. The Prescriptions area provides the same separate navigation for Normal Prescriptions and Pediatric Prescriptions. Selecting a tab navigates to its corresponding page; it is not merely a visual label.

  • Keep one shared game catalogue and one shared prescription system. Category pages are views over the shared records, not separate duplicated systems.
  • Each game has a category used to determine where it appears. Wind Chime is currently specified as a Pediatric Sensory module; the full pediatric catalogue can be defined as the product evolves.
  • Prescription pages display prescriptions and activities relevant to the selected category. A prescription that includes activities from both categories remains one prescription and may appear in both category views, with its identity and history preserved.
  • Existing game IDs, patient assignments, prescriptions, and completed-session history must remain compatible. Final routes should be chosen after reviewing the current application.

Situation

A doctor opens Pediatric Games to find a pediatric activity, then opens Pediatric Prescriptions to review assignments in that category. The doctor does not need to search through an undifferentiated list, and Candela does not maintain a separate prescription engine for pediatric patients.

14Hospital-entered measurement records and graphs

Process

Candela should support hospitals recording values collected during their own workflows, retaining those records over time, and presenting graphs to authorized users. The detailed entry model is intentionally not finalized; do not build around a fixed vendor, vendor-specific terminology, or a presumed universal metric template.

  • Hospital workflow: an authorized doctor or staff member enters the observed values against the appropriate patient and date, with enough context to identify what was measured and under which conditions.
  • Persistence: once saved, a record remains stored regardless of the patient's subscription status. Subscription expiry must not delete, reset, or prevent authorized hospital staff from maintaining the record.
  • Patient visibility: the patient-facing view of these records and graphs is governed separately by the agreed subscription-entitlement policy and applicable access rights. The system must not confuse hiding a premium view with deleting or withholding the underlying record from authorized care staff.
  • Hospital variation: hospitals may use different equipment, activities, measurement names, calculations, and graph axes. The eventual design must accommodate this variation without hardcoding any one external system's name or metric definitions.
  • Historical integrity: preserve the entered values, units and measurement context. If definitions or calculations are later changed, do not silently reinterpret previously saved records; any versioning and correction workflow will be specified during detailed design.
  • Graphs: the eventual feature should support viewing results over time and revisiting saved graphs. Calculations, averages, comparisons, and axis rules must be defined from the actual workflow before implementation; do not invent formulas.
  • Relationship and permissions: records are linked to the patient, not owned by a particular doctor. Access follows current authorization and applicable historical-access rules.
  • Separation from game analytics: manually entered measurements remain distinguishable from Candela's automatically captured game-session metrics. Combined timelines may be considered later, but metrics must not be treated as equivalent unless their definitions support comparison.

Situation

A doctor records a patient's measurement after an in-clinic activity. Candela saves the entry and retains it if the patient's subscription later expires. An authorized doctor can continue to manage the record; the patient sees only the record views allowed by the product's finalized entitlement and access rules. The same workflow can be configured for different hospital practices without naming or assuming one external system.

Design boundary — intentionally open

Do not finalize templates, folders, metric groups, custom form builders, graph formulas, or the exact configuration workflow yet. First confirm the real hospital data-entry process and examples. The durable requirements are record persistence, hospital variation, patient-linked history, clear data provenance, and separate subscription-controlled patient presentation.

Open business decisions

Keep these decisions visible during BRD review. Do not treat them as finalized architecture.

  • Subscription plan tiers, pricing, renewal, and cancellation policy.
  • Payment provider and failed-payment grace period.
  • Which historical progress views remain available after subscription expiry.
  • Transfer-request expiry, cancellation, and competing-request handling.
  • Former-doctor access to historical patient records and any consent requirements.
  • Exact organization-level patient visibility and organization membership history.

The story in one line

A person joins Candela → receives the access they are eligible for → practises games → builds a continuous history → may change doctor or subscription status → keeps their identity and history throughout.

These are high-level business diagrams. Detailed data models and solution/technology architecture should follow after the business requirements are validated.

Shared analytics equations and rules

All percentages must use the denominator defined for that game and analytics scope. A session card, level trend and prescription-period report can intentionally use different scopes. The label must make the scope clear.

\[ \text{Accuracy (\%)} = \frac{\text{Correct}}{\text{Eligible attempts}} \times 100 \]
\[ \text{Eligible attempts} = \text{event counters included by that game's helper for the requested scope} \]
\[ \text{Weighted Bee path adherence (\%)} = \frac{\sum_i (\text{Path adherence}_i \times \text{Weight}_i)}{\sum_i \text{Weight}_i} \]
\[ \text{Reaction time (s)} = \frac{\sum \text{correct-response reaction times}}{\text{Number of correct responses with a recorded reaction time}} \]
\[ \text{Speed-and-accuracy} = \frac{\text{Accuracy (\%)}}{\text{Average reaction time (s)}} \]
\[ \text{Recorded duration} = \sum \text{completed trial durations} \]
\[ \text{Daily activity} = \text{count of saved sessions grouped by the patient's local calendar date} \]
\[ \text{Sessions per week} = \text{count of saved sessions in each Monday--Sunday local week} \]
\[ \text{Level identity} = (\mathrm{gameId},\ \mathrm{levelId}) \]
\[ \text{Aggregate accuracy (\%)} = \frac{\sum \text{correct counts}}{\sum \text{eligible attempt counts}} \times 100 \]

Null handling: if there are no valid observations or the denominator is zero, the metric is unavailable (null), not 0%. Do not convert missing data into a zero-performance result.

Mixed games: do not combine different games' accuracy, Bee path adherence, reaction time or duration into a single score. Mixed-game session analytics returns no combined totals.

Rounding: calculate from unrounded counts and values, then apply the display precision used by the relevant shared helper or UI. Do not round each session before longitudinal aggregation.

Time: daily and weekly groupings use the requested IANA timezone; weeks begin Monday. Date-range boundaries are interpreted in that timezone. The selected period must not silently include sessions outside the local date range.

Prescription attribution: session attribution uses the client completion timestamp with an explicit timezone offset or Z. It must be finite and no more than five minutes in the future at insertion. A session links only when exactly one prescription revision window matches the game, level and timestamp. Invalid stored links are not silently reassigned during reads.

Practice snapshot: lifetime saved-session count plus the newest saved session are used for the compact summary; the summary does not calculate a cross-game score.

QA master plan — game and analytics acceptance

Run automated tests, builds and typechecks during implementation. Perform the consolidated manual QA pass after all planned implementation work is complete. The following cases are acceptance tests, not a claim that manual QA has already passed.

A. Game execution and result correctness

  • For every game, complete a normal successful run and compare the result screen with the saved session payload and shared report output.
  • Exercise every response class the game supports: correct, wrong, miss, timeout, skipped, cancelled, incomplete and retried. Confirm unsupported classes are not fabricated.
  • Verify level selection, level identity, configuration changes, start, instruction and cancel flow, completion transition and result presentation.
  • Verify abandoning before completion does not create a completed-session row.
  • Verify one completed play creates exactly one saved session, including under double-submit and network retry.
  • Verify optional game detail is versioned, sanitized and bounded; malformed optional detail must not prevent saving the core completed session.

B. Literal metric equation tests

  • For each game and each scope (session card, level analytics, longitudinal trend and prescription period), write down the exact numerator and denominator from the shared helper.
  • Test numerator 0 with a positive denominator; numerator equal to denominator; numerator greater than zero with denominator 0; all timeouts; all misses; all wrong responses; and mixed response classes.
  • Assert the exact raw result before display rounding, then assert the displayed percentage and decimal precision.
  • Test aggregation across unequal session sizes to prove counts are summed before division, rather than averaging percentages.
  • Test null and no-valid-attempt behavior separately from a genuine 0% score.
  • Test Bee weighted rounds and the mismatched-round fallback; ensure no tap-count accuracy is substituted.
  • Test reaction time uses correct-response samples only; test an empty sample list with and without a saved average; verify seconds as the unit.
  • Test trial-duration games by summing trial durations and ensure the UI does not describe this as continuous app time.

C. Persistence, attribution and privacy

  • Save every supported game's detail and verify JSON version, required fields, sanitization and retrieval.
  • Send malformed optional detail and verify the core session still persists.
  • Retry with the same client event ID and verify the original row and prescription link are returned without duplicate insertion or re-attribution.
  • Test completion timestamps with explicit offsets, Z, a missing offset, an invalid date, non-finite values, and timestamps more than five minutes in the future.
  • Test a timestamp exactly at each prescription revision boundary, overlapping or missing revision windows, an empty assignment snapshot, and a game or level mismatch.
  • Verify patients receive only patient-safe assignment analytics; revision IDs, actor IDs, doctor IDs and internal reason codes are not exposed.
  • Verify doctor endpoints enforce current patient ownership and that changing doctors does not transfer historical access implicitly.

D. Longitudinal analytics and date filters

  • Verify grouping by (gameId, levelId); never merge two levels or two games.
  • Test IANA timezone day boundaries, daylight-saving transitions, Monday week start and sessions around midnight.
  • Test an empty date range, a one-day range, a reversed range, partial query parameters and an invalid timezone; invalid requests return 400 where specified.
  • Verify that no query parameters preserve the legacy response shape.
  • Test the 2,000 and 2,001 session boundaries; retain the newest 2,000 where capped, return truncation metadata and label incomplete totals.
  • Test that active days count saved rows only; missing days are not failures. Verify duration semantics for each game.
  • Verify an assignment period shows current activities with no sessions, historical activities assigned during the selected period, and no activity for an empty level assignment.
  • Verify the Direction Sense mixed / straighten alias consistently across display, assignment, attribution and period coverage.

E. UI, access and role-specific checks

  • The patient home summary shows the lifetime saved-session count and the newest game, level and date, not mixed-game accuracy, mean reaction, efficiency or other unsupported combined scores.
  • The doctor patient summary shows the saved-session count, last saved session and prescribed modules; no cross-game mean accuracy.
  • Switch rapidly between patients and verify old assignment data disappears immediately; delayed responses from the previous patient must not overwrite the new patient.
  • Verify loading and error states do not retain another patient's assignment data.
  • Verify patient and doctor date controls operate independently and both default to the documented 30-day range.
  • Verify trial and subscription gating, prescription permissions and doctor-patient ownership separately; one permission must not bypass another.
  • Verify mobile and web render the same metric labels, units, null states, truncation notices and date semantics.

F. Final manual QA evidence

  • Record device and browser, build or commit, test account role, game, level, test input, expected equation, actual result, saved payload reference and pass or fail.
  • Capture screenshots or video for each game result, patient summary, doctor summary, level trend, prescription period and empty, error and truncated states.
  • Run the PostgreSQL migration up and down and verify schema and index state; test prescription revision concurrency and patient-level locking against a real PostgreSQL instance.
  • Verify the full workflow from account login, through a game and completed session, to patient history, doctor analytics, prescription revision and date-filtered coverage.
  • Keep defects and deferred cases visible; do not mark manual QA complete until evidence is recorded.

01Rotatory Wheel

Documentation familyVisual tracking and target selection
Session behaviorSaved detail is stored with the completed session.

Game behavior and stored result

Saved detail is stored with the completed session. Target response classification: correct target hit, wrong visible target hit, miss on empty space, background or wheel, or timeout when the target expires without a correct response.

How to play

Bubbles ride around a spinning wheel. The task is to find the mark that matches the prompt and tap only that bubble.

  1. Watch the wheel. Keep your eyes on the bubbles as they turn. A letter, number, or color is shown, and it may also be spoken.
  2. Learn the motion first. The first three targets are practice. They are not scored. After the third tap, the bubbles rearrange into the layout used for the scored round.
  3. Find the match. Each scored letter or number appears once. Tap the bubble that matches the prompt.
  4. Finish the round. A finished round opens the results card. Clap along when that card opens. Leaving before the end does not save a session.

Do and don't

Do

  • Keep watching the wheel while it turns.
  • Tap only the bubble that matches the shown or spoken target.
  • Finish the scored round so the result can be saved.

Don't

  • Tap a different bubble. That is a wrong tap.
  • Tap empty space, the background, or the wheel itself. That is a miss.
  • Count the first three practice taps in the score.
  • Leave before the round finishes.

Metric equations

\[ \text{Session card accuracy (\%)} = \frac{\text{Correct}}{\text{Correct} + \text{Wrong} + \text{Miss} + \text{Timeout}} \times 100 \] \[ \text{Level accuracy (\%)} = \frac{\text{Correct}}{\text{Correct} + \text{Wrong} + \text{Miss}} \times 100 \]

Level analytics excludes timeouts.

What the numbers mean

  • Accuracy. The share of counted responses that hit the right target. On the session card the count includes wrong taps, misses, and timeouts. On the level page timeouts are left out. A letter found only after extra taps is not 100%. Higher is better.
  • Time to the correct hit. The average seconds until the correct bubble is tapped, including a correct tap that came after a wrong one. Wrong taps, misses, and timeouts are not in this average. Lower is better.
  • Efficiency. This game's accuracy percent divided by that average time in seconds. Use it when accuracy and speed disagree. It describes only Rotatory Wheel. Higher is better.
  • Wrong-tap rate. The share of attempts that hit a bubble that was not the target. Lower is better.
  • Miss rate. The share of attempts that tapped empty space, the background, or the wheel. Lower is better.
  • Time to finish. How long the sitting lasted, in seconds. This is not the time to the correct hit. A sitting that runs into the time cap stays long. Lower is better when accuracy holds.

Worked example

Example: 20 correct, 10 wrong, 0 misses, 0 timeouts → card = 20 ÷ 30 × 100 = 66.7%. Level example: 1 correct and 9 timeouts → 1 ÷ 1 × 100 = 100%.

QA test focus

  • Test target identity changes
  • Correct versus wrong visible target
  • Background or wheel miss
  • Timeout
  • Card-versus-level denominator
  • Zero valid level attempts
  • Repeated sessions aggregation
  • Detail persistence

02Bee Path

Documentation familyVisual tracking and target following
Session behaviorThe stored path-adherence value is the metric.

Game behavior and stored result

The stored path-adherence value is the metric. It is not calculated from correct, wrong, miss or timeout tap counts.

How to play

A bee flies along a line. The player traces that line with a finger and stays inside the path until the route ends.

  1. Find the path. The bee shows the route. The line may be straight, curved, zigzag, a wave, a spiral, branching, dotted, or a generated path, depending on the level.
  2. Stay on the line. Trace with a finger. The score is how much of the trace stays inside the corridor, not a count of taps.
  3. Finish the route. Reach the end of the path to complete the round. The results card then opens. Clap along when it does.

Do and don't

Do

  • Follow the bee and keep the finger on the drawn path.
  • Finish the route so the stored path-adherence value can be saved.
  • Read the result as path adherence.

Don't

  • Drift off the path. That lowers path adherence.
  • Treat the result as tap accuracy, or label it Accuracy.
  • Leave before the route is finished.

Metric equations

\[ \text{Weighted path adherence (\%)} = \frac{\sum_i (\text{Path adherence}_i \times \text{Weight}_i)}{\sum_i \text{Weight}_i} \] \[ \text{Weight}_i = \begin{cases} \text{completed rounds} & \text{when the saved count matches completed rounds} \\ 1 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases} \]

What the numbers mean

  • Path adherence. The stored percent of the trace that stayed inside the corridor. It is not rounds divided by taps. Higher is better.
  • Time to first grab. The average seconds from the round being ready until the first grab. Samples that are not a real time at or above zero are ignored. Lower is better.
  • Efficiency. Path adherence (%) divided by this game's mean time in seconds. It describes only Bee Path. Higher is better.
  • Time to finish. The mean length of the sitting, in seconds. It is not the time to the first grab. Lower is better when path adherence holds.

Bee Path does not show a wrong-tap rate or a miss rate. Those tap counts are not the score.

Worked example

Example: 100% over 2 rounds and 50% over 1 round → (100×2 + 50×1) ÷ 3 = 83.3%.

QA test focus

  • Verify the stored score is used
  • Valid round weighting
  • Mismatched round-count fallback
  • Missing score
  • Zero completed rounds
  • Percent bounds
  • The label says Path adherence
  • Do not label it Accuracy

How to play

One bubble is the target and it keeps moving. Other bubbles are decoys. The player tracks the target and taps it.

  2. Ignore decoys. Other bubbles are there to distract. Do not tap those.
  3. Tap the target. Keep tracking, then tap the real target. One session is 20 bubbles in a row.
  4. Finish the row. A finished session opens the results card. Clap along when it opens. The raw path of the bubble is not stored.

Do and don't

Do

  • Keep your eyes on the moving target.
  • Tap that target when you can reach it.
  • Complete the row of bubbles so the trial metrics can be saved.

Don't

  • Tap a decoy. That is a wrong tap.
  • Tap empty space. That is a miss.
  • Stop in the middle of the 20-bubble row if you want a saved session.

\[ \text{Duration contribution} = \sum_i \text{completed trial duration}_i \]

  • Efficiency. Pursuit accuracy (%) divided by that mean time in seconds. It is not a score to compare with another game. Higher is better.
  • Wrong-tap rate. The share of attempts that hit a decoy or another stimulus that was not the target. Lower is better.
  • Miss rate. The share of attempts that tapped empty space or the background. Lower is better.
  • Time to finish. The mean sitting length, in seconds. It is the sum of completed trial times, not a reconstructed path, and not the time to the correct hit. Lower is better when accuracy holds.
